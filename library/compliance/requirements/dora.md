# DORA — Digital Operational Resilience Act — requirements checklist
> Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj · Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (OJ L 333, 27.12.2022) · retrieved 2026-06-22 · EU regulation, directly applicable in all Member States — text © European Union, reuse permitted with attribution (Decision 2011/833/EU). Adopted 14 Dec 2022, entered into force 16 Jan 2023, **applies from 17 January 2025**; supplemented by ESA RTS/ITS — e.g. RTS (EU) 2024/1772 (incident classification), RTS (EU) 2025/301 (incident-reporting content + time limits), ITS (EU) 2025/302 (reporting templates), plus RTS/ITS on ICT risk-management tools, the Register of Information, and TLPT (Commission Delegated/Implementing Regulations, 2024–2025). Reference only — not legal advice. Maps to the unified primitives in docs/reference/compliance-requirements.md.

## Applies when
- **Directly:** you are an EU "financial entity" under Art. 2 — credit institution; payment institution; e-money institution; account information service provider; investment firm; crypto-asset service provider or issuer of asset-referenced tokens (under MiCA); central securities depository; central counterparty; trading venue; trade repository; manager of alternative investment funds (AIFM); management company (UCITS); data reporting service provider; insurance or reinsurance undertaking; insurance/reinsurance/ancillary intermediary; institution for occupational retirement provision; credit rating agency; administrator of critical benchmarks; crowdfunding service provider; securitisation repository. A solo SaaS is normally **not** a financial entity.
- **Indirectly (the usual path for a managed-infra SaaS):** you are an **ICT third-party service provider (ICT TPP)** under Art. 3(19). DORA does not regulate you directly unless you are designated a *critical* ICT TPP (CTPP) under the Union Oversight Framework (Art. 31), but your financial-entity customers are legally required to flow DORA obligations down to you by contract (Art. 28–30). Selling ICT services to one EU bank / insurer / payment or investment firm / crypto-asset provider triggers this — treat it as a **sales-unlock track** activated by your first regulated customer.
- **What flows down to you contractually:** the Art. 30 key contractual provisions (audit & access rights, SLAs with quantitative targets, sub-contracting/sub-processor disclosure and conditions, data location, incident-notification timelines, assistance during ICT incidents, exit/termination support). Your customer must also include you in their Register of Information (Art. 28) and may include your systems in scope for their resilience testing (Art. 24–26) and TLPT (Art. 26–27).
- **Effective dates:** main obligations apply from **17 Jan 2025**. RTS/ITS effective dates vary (most in force during 2024–2025); the first Register-of-Information reporting cycle and the staggered designation of CTPPs run through 2025–2026. Whether a given clause binds you depends on the contract — have counsel review flow-down terms, audit-rights scope, sub-contracting conditions, and any cross-border data-location commitments.

## Requirements
### Art. 5 — Governance and organisation
> The management body of the financial entity shall define, approve, oversee and be responsible for the implementation of all arrangements related to the ICT risk management framework … bear the ultimate responsibility for managing the financial entity's ICT risk … set clear roles and responsibilities for all ICT-related functions … and maintain sufficient knowledge and skills to understand and assess ICT risk (Art. 5(2)).
- **Requires:** the management body owns, approves, oversees and is accountable for the ICT risk-management framework; sets risk tolerance, approves policies on ICT third-party arrangements, and maintains its own ICT-risk knowledge. Non-delegable to IT.
- **In code:** for an ICT TPP this surfaces as the customer demanding named ownership and evidence of governance — document a security owner, an ICT-risk policy, and a board/owner-level review cadence; expose it in your security addendum and audit responses.
- **Primitive:** 6. Immutable audit logging (governance/decision records); 9. Vendor / sub-processor register (your own posture as a vendor).
- **Cite:** Art. 5.
### Art. 6 — ICT risk-management framework
> Financial entities shall have a sound, comprehensive and well-documented ICT risk management framework … which enables them to address ICT risk quickly, efficiently and comprehensively … The ICT risk management framework shall include … a digital operational resilience strategy … be documented and reviewed at least once a year … as well as upon the occurrence of major ICT-related incidents … [and] be subject to internal audit by auditors on a regular basis (Art. 6(1)-(6)).
- **Requires:** a documented, comprehensive ICT risk-management framework with a digital-operational-resilience strategy, reviewed at least yearly and after major incidents; maps ICT assets and their dependencies; audited periodically by independent reviewers.
- **In code:** maintain a written framework keyed to your data inventory and asset map (4); schedule the annual review as a recurring task; retain audit/review artefacts.
- **Primitive:** 4. Data inventory + sensitive-field tagging; 6. Immutable audit logging.
- **Cite:** Art. 6.
### Art. 7 — ICT systems, protocols and tools
> In order to address and manage ICT risk, financial entities shall use and maintain updated ICT systems, protocols and tools that are: (a) appropriate to the magnitude of operations … (b) reliable; (c) equipped with sufficient capacity … (d) technologically resilient … (Art. 7).
- **Requires:** use ICT systems, protocols and tools that are reliable, have sufficient capacity, are technologically resilient, and are kept up to date.
- **In code:** patch/dependency currency via the security CI (dep-audit / SBOM in templates/ci/security.yml); capacity and resilience inherited from managed infra (Fly, Cloudflare, Supabase/Postgres) — document the SLAs you rely on.
- **Primitive:** 1. Encryption (TLS/key currency); 4. Data inventory.
- **Cite:** Art. 7.
### Art. 8 — Identification
> As part of the ICT risk management framework … financial entities shall identify, classify and adequately document all ICT supported business functions, roles and responsibilities, the information assets and ICT assets supporting those functions, and their roles and dependencies in relation to ICT risk … review … at least yearly … identify all ICT third-party service providers supporting critical or important functions (Art. 8(1)-(5)).
- **Requires:** identify, classify and document all ICT-supported business functions, information assets and ICT assets, their dependencies and the third parties supporting critical/important functions; maintain inventories and review risk on critical assets at least yearly.
- **In code:** the data inventory + sensitive-field tagging is the asset register; include third-party/sub-processor dependencies (your own vendors: Supabase, Fly, Cloudflare, Postgres host) so the map is complete.
- **Primitive:** 4. Data inventory + sensitive-field tagging; 9. Vendor / sub-processor register.
- **Cite:** Art. 8.
### Art. 9 — Protection and prevention
> Financial entities shall continuously monitor and control the security and functioning of ICT systems and tools and … minimise the impact of ICT risk … by deploying appropriate ICT security tools, policies and procedures … [to] ensure the security of the means of transfer of data; minimise the risk of corruption or loss of data … and prevent the lack of availability … of … data … design and implement … policies and protocols for strong authentication mechanisms … and … access management rights … on a need-to-know, need-to-use and least privilege basis (Art. 9(2)-(4)).
- **Requires:** continuously protect ICT systems; minimise attack surface; ensure security of data in transit, at rest and in use; enforce strong authentication, access rights on a need-to-know/least-privilege basis, and segregation of duties; deploy patching and change management.
- **In code:** TLS 1.2+ in transit and AES-256 at rest; Postgres RLS + RBAC, Supabase Auth with MFA, least-privilege service roles, key management. Shares its control surface with GDPR Art. 32 and HIPAA §164.312 — build once, map to many.
- **Primitive:** 1. Encryption (TLS 1.2+, AES-256, key management); 5. Access control — RLS / RBAC / MFA / least-privilege.
- **Cite:** Art. 9.
### Art. 10 — Detection
> Financial entities shall have in place mechanisms to promptly detect anomalous activities … including ICT network performance issues and ICT-related incidents, and to identify potential material single points of failure … [the] detection mechanisms … shall enable multiple layers of control, define alert thresholds and criteria to trigger and initiate ICT-related incident response processes (Art. 10(1)-(2)).
- **Requires:** mechanisms to promptly detect anomalous activity, ICT-related incidents and single points of failure; multiple layers of control; defined alert thresholds and criteria triggering incident-response processes.
- **In code:** logging/alerting on auth and data-access anomalies (Cloudflare, Supabase logs, app audit log); define alert thresholds that page the responder.
- **Primitive:** 6. Immutable audit logging; 8. Incident-response + breach pipeline.
- **Cite:** Art. 10.
### Art. 11 — Response and recovery
> … financial entities shall put in place a comprehensive ICT business continuity policy … [and] implement … (a) ICT response and recovery plans … the financial entities shall … estimate preliminary impacts, damages and losses … set out recovery plans … [and] test the ICT business continuity plans and the ICT response and recovery plans at least yearly (Art. 11(1)-(6)).
- **Requires:** an ICT business-continuity policy plus response-and-recovery plans; objectives that ensure continuity of critical/important functions; estimate impacts/losses; document and test the plans.
- **In code:** a written BC/DR plan with RTO/RPO targets leaning on managed-infra failover (Fly regions, Postgres PITR, Cloudflare); test it on a schedule and log the test.
- **Primitive:** 7. Retention + deletion jobs (backup lifecycle); 8. Incident-response + breach pipeline.
- **Cite:** Art. 11.
### Art. 12 — Backup policies and procedures, restoration and recovery procedures and methods
> … financial entities shall develop and document: (a) backup policies and procedures specifying the scope of the data that is subject to the backup and the minimum frequency of the backup, based on the criticality of information or the confidentiality level of the data; (b) restoration and recovery procedures and methods. Financial entities shall set up backup systems … [and] when restoring backup data … financial entities shall use ICT systems that are physically and logically segregated from the source ICT system (Art. 12(1)-(3)).
- **Requires:** backup policies specifying scope and frequency by data criticality; restoration/recovery procedures and methods; periodic testing of restoration; where needed, segregated/redundant capacity; secured backups.
- **In code:** Postgres point-in-time recovery / automated backups (Supabase/managed Postgres), encrypted at rest, restoration tested periodically and the test logged.
- **Primitive:** 7. Retention + deletion jobs; 1. Encryption (backup encryption).
- **Cite:** Art. 12.
### Art. 13 — Learning and evolving
> Financial entities shall have in place capabilities and staff to gather information on vulnerabilities and cyber threats, ICT-related incidents … and to analyse the likely impacts … [they] shall put in place post ICT-related incident reviews after a major ICT-related incident … [and] develop ICT security awareness programmes and digital operational resilience training as compulsory modules in their staff training schemes (Art. 13(1)-(6)).
- **Requires:** capture and analyse information on incidents and the threat landscape, run post-incident reviews, feed lessons back into the framework, and maintain staff security awareness/training.
- **In code:** structured post-incident review in the incident pipeline; retain root-cause and remediation records; lightweight training notes for a small team.
- **Primitive:** 8. Incident-response + breach pipeline; 6. Immutable audit logging.
- **Cite:** Art. 13.
### Art. 14 — Communication
> As part of the ICT risk management framework … financial entities shall have in place crisis communication plans enabling a responsible disclosure of, at least, major ICT-related incidents or vulnerabilities to clients and counterparts as well as to the public, as appropriate … [and] implement communication policies for internal staff and for external stakeholders … [designating] at least one person … in charge of implementing the communication strategy for ICT-related incidents (Art. 14(1)-(3)).
- **Requires:** crisis-communication plans for responsible disclosure of incidents/vulnerabilities to clients, counterparties and the public as relevant; a designated person/role for press and authority communications.
- **In code:** a templated customer/authority notification flow wired into the incident pipeline; named contact for breach communications in the security addendum.
- **Primitive:** 8. Incident-response + breach pipeline.
- **Cite:** Art. 14.
### Art. 16 — Simplified ICT risk-management framework
> [Certain smaller and lower-risk financial entities listed in Art. 16(1)] shall … put in place and maintain a sound and documented ICT risk management framework … [which shall] continuously monitor the security and functioning of all ICT systems; minimise the impact of ICT risk through the use of sound, resilient and updated ICT systems … identify … sources of ICT risk … set up business continuity plans … [and] implement … measures, … and processes designed to ensure recovery (Art. 16(1)).
- **Requires:** a proportionate, simplified framework for the smaller/lower-risk entities listed in Art. 16(1) (covering governance, protection, detection, continuity, third-party monitoring, and review). Note: this applies to qualifying *financial entities*, not to ICT TPPs.
- **In code:** if you are ever in scope as a small financial entity, the same primitives (4/5/6/7/8/9) satisfy the simplified set at reduced depth.
- **Primitive:** 4–9 (proportionate subset).
- **Cite:** Art. 16.
### Art. 17 — ICT-related incident management process
> Financial entities shall define, establish and implement an ICT-related incident management process to detect, manage and notify ICT-related incidents … [recording] all ICT-related incidents and significant cyber threats … establish appropriate procedures and processes to ensure a consistent and integrated monitoring, handling and follow-up … to ensure that root causes are identified, documented and addressed in order to prevent the occurrence of such incidents (Art. 17(1)-(3)).
- **Requires:** a process to detect, manage, log and notify ICT-related incidents; assign roles; record all incidents and their handling; track root causes to prevent recurrence.
- **In code:** an incident register with timestamps, severity, and resolution; the immutable audit log backs the record.
- **Primitive:** 8. Incident-response + breach pipeline; 6. Immutable audit logging.
- **Cite:** Art. 17.
### Art. 18 — Classification of ICT-related incidents and cyber threats
> Financial entities shall classify ICT-related incidents … on the basis of the following criteria: (a) the number and/or relevance of clients or financial counterparts affected … (b) the duration of the ICT-related incident … (c) the geographical spread … (d) the data losses … (e) the criticality of the services affected … (f) the economic impact … (Art. 18(1)).
- **Requires:** classify incidents by the Art. 18 criteria — number/relevance of clients and counterparties affected, data losses, duration/service downtime, geographical spread, reputational impact, economic impact, and criticality of services affected — to determine which are *major*. (Thresholds detailed in the RTS on classification.)
- **In code:** encode these criteria as a severity-scoring step in the incident pipeline so "major" is triggered consistently and auditable.
- **Primitive:** 8. Incident-response + breach pipeline.
- **Cite:** Art. 18.
### Art. 19 — Reporting of major ICT-related incidents and voluntary notification of significant cyber threats
> Financial entities shall report major ICT-related incidents to the relevant competent authority … [and] shall submit … (a) an initial notification; (b) an intermediate report … as soon as the status of the original incident has changed significantly …; (c) a final report, when the root cause analysis has been completed … Financial entities may, on a voluntary basis, notify significant cyber threats to the relevant competent authority … Where a major ICT-related incident … has an impact on the financial interests of clients, financial entities shall, without undue delay … inform their clients (Art. 19(1)-(4)).
- **Requires:** report **major** ICT-related incidents to the competent authority via staged **initial / intermediate / final** reports on the timelines and templates set by the ESAs (ITS/RTS under Art. 20); affected clients notified without undue delay where there is impact; *voluntary* notification of significant cyber threats is permitted.
- **In code:** as an ICT TPP your contract (Art. 30) obliges you to notify the financial-entity customer fast enough for them to meet these deadlines — build a timed, templated breach-notification path. The staged deadlines themselves are set by the reporting-content RTS (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/301, Art. 5), with harmonised templates in ITS (EU) 2025/302 — not the Art. 19 base text: **initial notification** no later than 24h after the entity becomes aware of the major incident (and within 4h of classifying it as major), **intermediate report** within 72h of the initial notification, **final report** no later than 1 month after the latest intermediate report. Treat these as RTS-derived figures. Shares mechanics with GDPR Art. 33 and NIS2 reporting.
- **Primitive:** 8. Incident-response + breach pipeline; 6. Immutable audit logging (notification trail).
- **Cite:** Art. 19 (reporting obligation); staged deadlines RTS (EU) 2025/301 Art. 5; templates ITS (EU) 2025/302 (both empowered by Art. 20).
### Art. 24–25 — Digital operational resilience testing programme
> … financial entities … shall establish, maintain and review a sound and comprehensive digital operational resilience testing programme … [which] shall provide … for the execution of appropriate tests, such as vulnerability assessments and scans, open source analyses, network security assessments, gap analyses, physical security reviews, questionnaires and scanning software solutions, source code reviews where feasible, scenario-based tests, compatibility testing, performance testing, end-to-end testing and penetration testing … [financial entities] shall ensure … tests are undertaken … at least yearly on all ICT systems and applications supporting critical or important functions (Art. 24(1)-(6), Art. 25(1)-(2)).
- **Requires:** a risk-based testing programme covering vulnerability assessments/scans, open-source and network security analyses, gap analyses, physical-security reviews, source-code reviews where feasible, scenario-based and penetration testing; ICT systems supporting critical/important functions tested at least yearly; vulnerabilities remediated.
- **In code:** dep-audit, SBOM generation and secret scanning (gitleaks) in templates/ci/security.yml deliver continuous vulnerability/source-code assurance; schedule annual pen-testing and track remediation. As an ICT TPP, your customer may pull your systems into their test scope — keep findings/remediation evidence shareable.
- **Primitive:** 8. Incident-response + breach pipeline (vuln remediation); 6. Immutable audit logging.
- **Cite:** Art. 24, Art. 25.
### Art. 26–27 — Advanced testing (TLPT) and tester requirements
> Financial entities … identified [as significant] … shall carry out at least every 3 years advanced testing by means of threat-led penetration testing … [which] shall cover several or all critical or important functions of a financial entity, and shall be performed on live production systems … Financial entities shall contract ICT third-party service providers for … the TLPT … [and] use … testers [meeting the requirements of Art. 27, on suitability, independence and certification] (Art. 26(1)-(8), Art. 27(1)-(3)).
- **Requires:** identified significant financial entities perform **threat-led penetration testing at least every 3 years** on live production systems covering critical/important functions, including ICT TPPs that support them; testers meet the Art. 27 competence/independence criteria; results validated by the authority (TIBER-EU-aligned; detail in the TLPT RTS). This is an obligation on the *financial entity*, not the ICT TPP directly.
- **In code:** if a customer's TLPT scopes in the functions you provide, you must support red-team testing against production (or a faithful equivalent) and may need to co-sign scoping; retain attestations.
- **Primitive:** 8. Incident-response + breach pipeline; 5. Access control (test-environment isolation).
- **Cite:** Art. 26, Art. 27.
### Art. 28 — General principles for ICT third-party risk + Register of Information
> Financial entities shall manage ICT third-party risk as an integral component of ICT risk … [and] remain fully responsible for compliance … As part of their ICT risk management framework, financial entities shall maintain and update … a register of information in relation to all contractual arrangements on the use of ICT services provided by ICT third-party service providers … [distinguishing] those which cover ICT services supporting critical or important functions … [and report] at least yearly … the number of new arrangements … as well as … the register of information (Art. 28(1)-(3)).
- **Requires:** financial entities manage ICT third-party risk as part of the framework, remain fully responsible for compliance, and maintain a **Register of Information** on all contractual arrangements for ICT services (distinguishing those supporting critical/important functions), reported at least yearly to the competent authority; assess concentration risk before contracting.
- **In code:** you are an entry in your customer's Register — supply accurate identifiers (legal entity, LEI, service descriptions, data locations, sub-contractors). Maintain your *own* vendor/sub-processor register for the chain beneath you.
- **Primitive:** 9. Vendor / sub-processor register (BAA / DPA / security addendum).
- **Cite:** Art. 28.
### Art. 29 — Preliminary assessment of ICT concentration risk
> When … identifying and assessing [ICT concentration] risk, financial entities shall … take into account whether they would enter into a contractual arrangement … [where] the ICT third-party service provider is not easily substitutable; or … would result in having in place multiple contractual arrangements … with the same ICT third-party service provider … [and] shall … assess the benefits and costs of alternative solutions, such as the use of different ICT third-party service providers … [and] possible sub-outsourcing arrangements … (Art. 29(1)-(2)).
- **Requires:** before entering a contract for a critical/important function, assess concentration risk, sub-outsourcing chains, substitutability, and the difficulty of exit/reintegration.
- **In code:** disclose your full sub-processor chain and any single-provider dependencies (e.g. reliance on one cloud region) so the customer can assess concentration; surface this in the DPA/security addendum.
- **Primitive:** 9. Vendor / sub-processor register.
- **Cite:** Art. 29.
### Art. 30 — Key contractual provisions
> The contractual arrangements … shall include … (a) a clear and complete description of all functions and ICT services … (b) the locations … where … data is to be processed and stored … and the requirement to notify … if [they] change; (c) provisions on … data protection … (d) … guaranteeing access, recovery and return … of … data; (e) service level descriptions … (f) the obligation … to provide assistance [during] an ICT incident …; (g) … to fully cooperate with the competent authorities …; (h) termination rights … For [critical or important functions, the arrangements] shall also include … full service level descriptions … with precise quantitative and qualitative performance targets …; the … rights of access, inspection and audit by the financial entity, or an appointed third party, and by the competent authority …; exit strategies (Art. 30(2)-(3)).
- **Requires:** ICT contracts (especially for critical/important functions) must specify, among others: full service description and locations where data is processed/stored and any change notice; data protection/availability/integrity provisions; **access, inspection and audit rights** for the entity and the competent authority; SLAs with quantitative/qualitative performance targets; assistance during ICT incidents at no/predetermined cost; obligation to cooperate with authorities; **sub-contracting conditions and disclosure**; termination/exit rights and transition assistance; incident-reporting obligations.
- **In code:** map each clause to a control you can evidence — data-location and change-notice from your data inventory; audit rights honoured via logs/SOC-style reports; SLAs from managed-infra commitments; sub-processor disclosure from the vendor register; exit via documented data export (the DSAR/export engine doubles as bulk-export tooling). These are the clauses that actually flow down to you.
- **Primitive:** 9. Vendor / sub-processor register; 2. DSAR engine (export/portability for exit); 6. Immutable audit logging (audit-rights evidence).
- **Cite:** Art. 30.
### Art. 31–44 — Union Oversight Framework for critical ICT third-party providers (CTPPs)
> The ESAs … shall … designate the ICT third-party service providers that are critical for financial entities, taking into account [the systemic impact, the reliance of financial entities, the substitutability, and the number of Member States in which services are provided] … For each critical ICT third-party service provider, the … [Lead Overseer] shall be responsible for assessing whether [it] has in place comprehensive, sound and effective rules, procedures, mechanisms and arrangements to manage the ICT risk … [with] powers [to request information, conduct investigations and inspections, issue recommendations, and impose periodic penalty payments] (Art. 31(1)-(2), Art. 33, Art. 35).
- **Requires:** the ESAs designate *critical* ICT TPPs (Art. 31), establish the Oversight Framework structure and a Lead Overseer (Art. 32–33), and grant oversight/information/investigation/inspection powers, recommendations, and penalties for non-cooperation (Art. 35–44). Designation depends on systemic importance, substitutability, and the number/significance of financial entities served — most SaaS vendors are **not** designated.
- **In code:** monitor whether you approach designation thresholds (broad systemic reliance by EU financial entities). If designated, you become directly subject to oversight, inspections, and information requests — a materially different compliance posture requiring legal counsel. Until then, this chapter is context, not an obligation on you.
- **Primitive:** 9. Vendor / sub-processor register; 6. Immutable audit logging (oversight evidence).
- **Cite:** Art. 31–44.
### Art. 45 — Information-sharing arrangements on cyber-threat information
> Financial entities may exchange amongst themselves cyber threat information and intelligence, including indicators of compromise, tactics, techniques, and procedures, cyber security alerts and configuration tools, to the extent that such information and intelligence sharing: (a) aims to enhance the digital operational resilience …; (b) takes place within trusted communities …; (c) is implemented through information-sharing arrangements that protect the potentially sensitive nature of the information … Financial entities shall notify competent authorities of their participation … upon validation of their membership (Art. 45(1)-(3)).
- **Requires:** financial entities **may** exchange cyber-threat information and intelligence (indicators, TTPs, alerts) within trusted communities, under arrangements that protect confidentiality and personal data; participation is voluntary and authorities are notified.
- **In code:** optional; if you join a sharing community, ensure shared data is sanitised and any personal data handled lawfully (ties to GDPR).
- **Primitive:** 8. Incident-response + breach pipeline; 3. Consent + preference store (lawful basis for any personal data shared).
- **Cite:** Art. 45.

## Evidence to retain
- **ICT risk-management framework** document, digital-operational-resilience strategy, and the dated annual-review/board-approval records (Art. 5, 6).
- **Asset / data inventory** mapping ICT assets, business functions, dependencies, and third parties supporting critical/important functions (Art. 8).
- **Security control evidence:** encryption config (TLS, at-rest), access-control/RLS/RBAC/MFA policies, least-privilege role definitions, patch/change records (Art. 7, 9).
- **Detection & logging:** alerting thresholds, immutable audit logs of access and security events (Art. 10).
- **Business-continuity & backup:** BC/DR plan with RTO/RPO, backup policy, and **dated restoration-test results** (Art. 11, 12).
- **Incident records:** incident register with classification scoring, timelines, root-cause and remediation, and the **initial/intermediate/final** notification trail for any major incident (Art. 17, 18, 19).
- **Testing evidence:** resilience-testing programme results (vuln scans, SBOM, gitleaks/dep-audit outputs, annual pen-test reports) and remediation tracking; TLPT attestations where you were in scope (Art. 24–27).
- **Vendor / contractual evidence:** your own sub-processor register; Register-of-Information data points you supply to customers; executed contracts evidencing the Art. 30 key provisions (audit rights, SLAs, sub-contracting disclosure, data location, exit/transition, incident-notification timelines) (Art. 28–30).
- **Communication artefacts:** crisis-communication plan and named breach-notification contact (Art. 14).
- Retain in line with each customer contract and applicable financial-sector record-keeping rules; DORA itself defers specific retention periods to the contract, the RTS, and the supervising authority. Have counsel confirm flow-down terms, audit-rights scope, sub-contracting conditions, cross-border data-location commitments, and any CTPP-designation exposure.
